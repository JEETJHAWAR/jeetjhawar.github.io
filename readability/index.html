    How it works.

    Classic readability formulas, run in plain JavaScript on your own device. Here is what each part of the report means.

    Readability score, 0–100

    The dial blends six classic formulas (Flesch, Fog, SMOG and others) into one score. They look at sentence length, long words and words most readers don't know. Names, numbers and links don't count against you. Higher is easier; most web writing should land above 60.

    Five grade formulas, one answer

    Flesch–Kincaid, Gunning Fog, SMOG, Coleman–Liau and ARI each estimate the school grade a reader needs. They disagree a little, so Readable takes the middle one as the consensus grade.

    How the hardest are picked

    Every sentence gets its own difficulty from its length, its long and complex words, and patterns such as passive voice. The top five are ranked, explained, and paired with concrete fixes.

    What the colours mean

    • Faint yellow an OK sentence
    • Yellow a hard one
    • Coral very hard
    • teal dots passive voice
    • coral wave complex word
    • grey dashes adverb or filler

    Nothing leaves your browser.

    There is no server, no account and no tracking of what you paste. Your text is analysed on this device and forgotten when you close the tab. Shared links carry only a score.
